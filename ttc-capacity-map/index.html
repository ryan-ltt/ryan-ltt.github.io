      How this map is built

      Method & sources

      What a line's width measures

      Scheduled capacity in the chosen hour: the most passengers a route's timetable can move past its busiest point in that clock hour, at the planning load for each vehicle. It is not ridership, and it is not a crush-load maximum.

      capacity (pph) = Σ trips departing the busiest stop in that hour × planning load of each trip's vehicle
      • Busiest point. For each hour, every stop in both directions is checked and the highest total wins — where branches and short-turns overlap.
      • Days. Real sample days inside every feed's validity window: Tuesday Sept 22, Saturday Sept 26 and Sunday Sept 27, 2026.
      • Late night. After about 1 AM, daytime routes show only their last trips finishing their runs. Overnight service is the TTC's Blue Night network and a few regional night routes.
      • All routes. The fourth button on the time bar drops the hour and draws every route at its own busiest hour, so peak-only and school routes are not hidden. Each route is then at a different moment, so the map is a catalogue rather than a snapshot.
      • Where the lines stop. Routes are drawn only inside Ontario — VIA reaches Vancouver and New York, Ontario Northland reaches Winnipeg — but every figure counts the whole trip.

      Passengers per vehicle

      Every trip is counted at its vehicle’s peak planning load, all day, so width reflects how often vehicles come and how big they are. Where an agency publishes a loading standard, that standard is used. Where none exists, the TTC's Service Standards figure for the same vehicle type is applied so every line on the map is measured the same way. The route panel names the basis for each route.

      How each agency's vehicles are assigned

      • TTC: the Service Summary lists the vehicle for every branch and period, so articulated buses, streetcars and bus-replaced streetcar routes are counted exactly.
      • GO rail — the biggest estimate on the map. Metrolinx publishes no per-trip consist, and no unaffiliated dataset of GO train lengths exists. The map assumes 12 coaches in peak and 10 off-peak (Metrolinx calls 12 cars its standard train and lengthened Lakeshore trips from 10 to 12 in 2022), and 8 on Richmond Hill, whose trains run "six to ten coaches". Coaches seat 136–162 depending on series and this uses 162, the top of the range, so GO figures are more likely high than low — each train's range is shown in the route panel. Metrolinx’s open API does expose real composition through its Fleet/Consist endpoint, which needs a free API key.
      • GO bus and UP Express: counted at seated capacity — 57 per coach, 173 per UP train.
      • YRT: Viva routes counted as 60-foot articulated, others as 40-foot, using YRT’s own Table 11 figures. YRT’s XD60 artics also work some conventional routes, so those are understated.
      • Brampton: Züm routes (501, 502, 505, 511) counted as articulated. Brampton runs 40-foot buses on Züm too, and its realtime feed needs an API key, so these four routes are overstated.
      • VIA Rail: Corridor trains are counted as one Siemens Venture set — 281 seats, the configuration VIA publishes. Which set works which train is not published, and the Canadian and the Maple Leaf run seasonal consists, so those are a flat estimate.
      • Ontario Northland: the Northlander at its published 169 seats; the motor coaches at an estimated 55, since ONTC publishes no seat count.
      • O-Train and ION: neither operator publishes a planning load, so the TTC's streetcar rule is applied to the capacity each does publish — peak at 52% of it, off-peak at the seat count. A two-car Citadis Spirit on O-Train Line 1 comes out at 312.
      • MiWay, Hamilton and Durham: none publishes an articulated assignment, so the map watches their realtime feeds. Every vehicle broadcasts its fleet number, and the published rosters say which numbers are 60-foot artics. Where a route runs a mix, it is counted at the observed proportion rather than all-or-nothing — Durham’s eight artics work PULSE 900 and 901, but most trips there are still 40-foot buses. The route panel shows the share and how many sightings it rests on.

      Limits worth knowing

      • Scheduled service, not delivered service.
      • Routes sharing a street are not added together; each is drawn on its own, thicker routes underneath.
      • Feeds come from forty agencies with different update cycles, so some schedules are fresher than others.
      • Buses outside the GTHA are counted as 40-foot buses. No agency east or west of the Toronto region publishes an articulated assignment, and their realtime feeds either need a key or do not exist, so OC Transpo, Grand River Transit, London and the rest are understated wherever they run 60-foot or double-decker buses. OC Transpo, which runs a lot of both, is the largest of these gaps.
      • Membership was tested rather than assumed: every candidate agency's stops were matched against the stops of GO, UP, the TTC, VIA and Ontario Northland. Kawartha Lakes and St. Thomas came out 27 km and 21 km from anything Toronto-bound, so they are not on the map.
      • School and seasonal routes appear only if they run on the sample days. Hamilton lists 80 routes but runs 31 on a normal weekday.
      • GO's seated-capacity basis is not comparable to the TTC's standing-load standards: a GO train counted at seats only still dwarfs a bus counted with standees.
