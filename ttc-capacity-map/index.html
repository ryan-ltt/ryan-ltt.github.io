      How this map is built

      Method & sources

      What a line's width measures

      Scheduled capacity in the chosen hour: the most passengers a route's timetable can move past its busiest point in that clock hour, at the TTC's own planning load for each vehicle. It is not ridership, and it is not a crush-load maximum.

      capacity (pph) = Σ trips departing the busiest stop in that hour × planning load of each trip's vehicle
      • Busiest point. For each hour, every stop in both directions is checked and the highest total wins. That is where branches and short-turns overlap, which is the TTC's "maximum load point" idea applied to supply.
      • Days. Each day type is a real sample day in the schedule's validity window, avoiding holidays: Tuesday Sept 15, Saturday Sept 19 and Sunday Sept 20, 2026.
      • Late night. After about 1 AM, subway and daytime routes show only their last trips finishing their runs, so they look thin before disappearing. Overnight service is the Blue Night network, which you can turn on under Networks.

      Passengers per vehicle

      Vehicle types come from the TTC Service Summary, branch by branch and period by period (so an articulated bus in the AM peak and a 12 m bus on Sunday are counted correctly, and streetcar routes temporarily run by buses are counted as buses). Loads come from the TTC Service Standards, Table 4.

      Derived The TTC has not published crowding standards for Line 5 or Line 6, so these apply the streetcar standard's ratios. The Flexity Outlook streetcar's 130-passenger peak standard is 52% of its 251 crush load, and its 70-passenger off-peak standard equals its 70 seats.

      • Line 5 (Flexity Freedom, 2-car trains): 251 crush per car × 0.52 ≈ 130 per car, so 260 per train peak. Off-peak uses 70 seats per car (assumed equal to Flexity Outlook), so 140 per train.
      • Line 6 (48 m Citadis Spirit, single vehicles): 336 maximum × 0.52 ≈ 174 peak; 120 seats off-peak.
      • Community bus: 11 passengers, per the TTC's Community Bus page.
      • Line 2 is counted as T1 trains (1,000) and Line 1 as Toronto Rocket trains (1,100).

      Two ways to count a vehicle

      Peak standard (default) counts every trip at its vehicle's peak load, so the width reflects only how often vehicles come and how big they are. By time of day follows the Service Standards literally: peak loads 6–9 AM and 3–7 PM on weekdays, and the lower off-peak comfort standard at all other times.

      Limits worth knowing

      • Scheduled service, not delivered service. Gaps, bunching, diversions and unscheduled extras aren't reflected.
      • Routes sharing a street are not added together. Each route is drawn on its own, with thicker routes underneath.
      • Hourly counts move by ±1 vehicle when a headway doesn't divide evenly into 60 minutes.
      • Line 5 opened February 8, 2026 and Line 6 on December 7, 2025. Both are still settling in, and their schedules may change.
      • The Service Summary covers the full length of each branch. Trips in the GTFS feed without a branch letter take the vehicle type used on that route in the same period.
