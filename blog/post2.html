people don't really care if their media is ai-generated

2026-11-16

If you spend any amount of time on social media, you have probably noticed the rise of AI slop in your feed. Restaurant menus, so called “realistic” deepfakes of celebrities saying ridiculous things, or just straight up AI-fruit tiktok dramas?

However you feel about AI generally, you can probably agree that this type of content is not good. It’s not particularly entertaining or stimulating, it has that distinct AI look where the text is all fucked up and the images are borderline uncanny valley. That type of AI generated content is not really what this essay is about. I know elderly people love it because I can hear my grandma absentmindedly watching that kind of stuff on tiktok all the time, but for the vast majority of young, thinking people, that kind of content is easy to spot and push back on, even when absentminded. This essay is really about a new kind of AI-generated content that is harder to detect and actually has a somewhat coherent storyline that you may mindlessly watch.

This type of AI-generated content has that typical AI-generated voiceover, but it’s getting good and harder to detect from real speech. The animation style does not ring as AI at first but once you watch a few you can tell it was AI-generated. It’s different from the usual slop, as it’s built from AI-generated code rather than stable diffusion style AI-generated images and videos. The content is stimulating as well, featuring interesting situations like surviving a zombie apocalypse, or presenting a problem and then building up the solution step by step until you have a real concept. The linked example is one that demonstrates how pub golf works. If you dig into the accounts that post these videos, they’re very obviously content farms that pump out tons of content like this, with 3-4 being posted a day. Real animation studios would never focus on such low level and relatively uninteresting videos like this, but with a Claude Max subscription and a good set of skills, you can easily churn out a few of these videos in a day with less than an hour of prompting.

I’ve known of this content for a while, but I never really found it to be too engaging until I let my guard down. Like many other people, my position on AI was that it was really useful for things like coding, but in realms like art and content creation, I value the fact that the media I consume was created by a human. Currently, I would not support AI-generated movies, music, or YouTube videos. However a week or two ago, I was doomscrolling and was zoned out, and realized that I was multiple minutes into one of these AI-generated videos. It didn’t automatically register to me as AI-generated, and it hit the threshold of being entertaining enough for me not to scroll past.

This brings me to the main point, which is that many people like myself are ideologically opposed to AI-generated media, and their beliefs are reinforced by a lot of the slop that you have seen online for the past few years. You can see that the media is bad, it’s low effort, and it’s not cohesive. This aligns with your beliefs that AI-generated media is bad, not just immoral, but it’s bad in the way that it doesn’t make sense and doesn’t look right. It lacks something that makes it truly entertaining and engagable. However, with the influx of this new AI-generated content online, and the rate of improvement of AI models, there is going to be one time when you’re tired, when you’re mindlessly scrolling, and when the video is better than your threshold detecting it as AI-generated. You’re gonna fall for it.

Maybe you’re stronger willed than I am and you vehemently reject all AI, and you consume media very intentionally and don’t doomscroll. That type of person is an ideal for many, including myself, but I think that it’s very hard to achieve in practice. Most people don’t think about their entertainment. They listen to whatever is popular on their Spotify mixes, they watch whatever is popular on Netflix, and they don’t really care to curate their entertainment. These people represent the majority of consumers, and they will be catered to by AI-generated content, if they are not already. The AI fruit drama that I linked earlier has 81.1 million views, so someone has to be watching it… This reality has been clear to me for a few years now. The actual issue is with the group of people who really do care about art. They are creative people and actually value their entertainment as something to be respected and enjoyed thoughtfully. I group myself in with these people, as I enjoy a movie that I have to think about, and I like music that is different from the mainstream and speaks to me personally. Yet I am still getting entertainment value out of AI-generated media that is barely above slop! If you allow yourself to consume mindlessly like many do today, you will end up finding value out of AI-generated media.

As this media gets better and more pervasive, you will slowly lower your guard. Maybe you won’t even be able to tell the difference between human-made and AI-generated media. At some point, whether you realize it or not, you will give up the fight against AI-generated content, and you will mindlessly scroll for hours and not feel any which way about it. You won’t need to sign up for gimmicky platforms like OpenAI tried with Sora last year, but it will slowly infect your Instagram Reels, your TikTok For You Page, your YouTube shorts. It will eventually pivot its way into longform content like YouTube video essays, and finally into the big media institutions, with AI-assisted movies appearing at first, maybe to help out with gruelling animation tasks, and then it will continue until major studios no longer need to pay talent to voice animated characters, or play out live action ones.

We give ourselves reasons today to reject AI-generated media, and they seem valid from the perspective of today, as when you extrapolate 5 or 10 years in the future like I just did, you can see the decline of human creativity, and human artforms becoming devoid of meaning. These trends are very visible in the long term. Living day to day, they blend in as a slow moving change. We see how bad AI slop is and how people could make a much more compelling piece of media if they just tried hard at it themselves. But when the output becomes more than you can guard against, the media becomes more engaging and realistic, some day you too will find yourself mindlessly scrolling AI-generated media, and not really finding an issue with it.
